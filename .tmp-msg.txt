feat: bigger CATEGORIES, an eye in its colours, and a header-wide fold target

Four legend changes, verified in a real browser at 1440px and 390px.

CATEGORIES is bigger (0.5 -> 0.58rem), and the responsive overrides were raised to
match rather than left to override it back down on a phone.

The eye now wears the caption's colours. Its strokes and iris are currentColor and
the button takes --fg-subtle, so the two read as one piece of chrome instead of a
white badge sitting on a muted label. Open and closed carry the same weight, because
the lid rotating over the iris is what states the condition; dimming the control as
well made the closed state look disabled rather than closed. The glyph is 0.74em, a
little under the caption, in both states. The button stays larger than the glyph so
it remains a tap target - the slack lives in the box, not in the drawing.

The fold target is the whole header row. Previously only the seven-pixel-tall word
was clickable, so the empty space in front of, above, under and behind it - which is
where a pointer actually goes - did nothing. worldmap.js now listens on the header
row, with padding that gives it real height. Keyboard focus and role=button stay on
the word itself, because nesting one button role inside another is invalid ARIA. The
eye stops propagation, so it keeps its own job.

The frame is wide enough that no label wraps: 292px, sized off "Ground Deployments &
Fleet Movements" at 36 characters. The right edge lands at 25.3% of the map, where
South America starts at 33.3%, and a test holds that line. Labels are nowrap with an
ellipsis fallback rather than wrapping the count onto a second line.

Two bugs found while testing this, both mine:

- I left a stopPropagation on the title, which meant clicking the word itself no
  longer folded anything - the very first thing the change was meant to make work.
- The fold test measured the header once and reused the coordinates, which went
  stale the moment folding rebuilt the legend and moved it. It re-measures before
  every click now.

Asserted as relationships rather than pixel thresholds (the caption is taller than
the eye, the row is taller than the caption), because a fixed threshold is only a
font-size assertion that breaks the first time a breakpoint scales it.
